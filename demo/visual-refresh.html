1 · The surface ladder, in context

This is the change that does the most work. Chrome, content and the page ground are now three distinct planes rather than one flat field. Look at where the sidebar ends and the content begins — before this change those were the same colour and the only thing separating them was a one-pixel border.

Before: --theme-body-bg and --theme-surface-bg both held #f8fafc. Identical. The page had no ground, so every panel floated on the same tone and the interface read as a diagram of itself.

2 · The four surface tokens

Ordered darkest to lightest in light mode: sunken, ground, chrome, content. The order inverts in dark mode — toggle the theme and the ladder still reads, because elevation lightens on a dark ground and darkens on a light one.

3 · Corner radius

4 · Shadow elevation

Each elevation is two layers — a tight key shadow that reads as contact, a wider ambient one that reads as distance — both at low alpha. The previous system used a single wide blur at higher opacity, which reads as a smudge under the element rather than the element lifting off the page.

5 · Tabs

The indicator is inset from the tab edges, so it marks the label rather than the whole cell. That single detail is most of what made the old strip read as a segmented control. It also now takes --theme-primary instead of a hardcoded blue, which is why it works in dark mode at all.

Click the tabs — the indicator cross-fades rather than jumping, because it is present on every tab and revealed by opacity on the active one.

6 · State layers

Hover and active are now translucent overlays rather than opaque greys. The same two tokens shade every row below, even though each sits on a different surface. An opaque grey would have punched a hole in three of the four.
